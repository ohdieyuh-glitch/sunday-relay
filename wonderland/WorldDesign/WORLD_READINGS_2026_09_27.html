Night, Water and Glow

The founder's night correction, and the three priorities measured underneath it. Two things run through all of it: 388 light fixtures were standing in the world throwing nothing, and several instruments were reporting confident numbers about things they were not pointed at. Neither failed, logged, nor went red. Both were found by chasing something else.

The night correction · Section B

Half the world’s light fixtures were switched off

audit_light_coveragefound and fixed
388 light fixtures standing in the world, throwing nothing — against 34 passes' worth that were working

They were physically there: above ground, z 12 to 211, spread from x −45,018 to 48,456. place_practical_lights.TAKE is a hand-maintained allowlist keyed by pass name, and a pass that is not a key contributes zero lights however many fixtures it holds. It listed eleven SD01_* tiles and six FD05_* and had fallen behind as more were authored — the whole Cx family, nine other Fill variants, and GF entirely.

186 of the 388 are waymarker lamps. The world’s wayfinding fixtures, unlit. The correction asks that the practicals show where traversal continues without the HUD, and the fixtures built for precisely that were the largest dark group.

The failure mode is worth more than the fix: a hand-maintained allowlist where the cost of an omission is silence. Nothing failed, nothing logged, no gate went red, and the fixtures looked entirely correct in daylight — they are ordinary meshes and they render. There is now a gate for it, and it reads the output: it counts fixtures in every layer spec against lights actually present in the light file. Its first run in that form found 91 more — a pass correctly listed in TAKE that still emits nothing, because its wall lanterns are glass-only and only the gold part emits. A membership check would have called that correct forever.

The night correction · Section A

The ground is not dark because of what it is made of

N_NOON_DARKmeasured

The ground reads 99.4% below the darkness threshold at deepest night, in six views across three different places — identical enough to be a property of the world rather than of any location. The same patches at noon:

The paving has ample albedo. It is not a dark material — a dark material would be dark at noon too. That rules out “darkest-material response” from the correction’s list of eleven systems by name, and narrows the cause to the ones that deliver light to a surface no lamp points at: indirect, local exposure and ambient. Which is exactly the group with no code touching it.

It also bounds what lamp placement can achieve, including my own. A lamp lights a pool around itself; 99.4% dark across three places is the space between the pools. The three nation networks will move the readings near the lamps and will not move that number. The floor has to come from section A, and the first piece of it — local exposure on the night shadow contrast — is written and not yet built.

Priorities 1 & 2 · Night readability

The sweep was photographing one camera and measuring another

N_SANITYinstrument fault, fixed

The night sweep saved its evidence from the composited game viewport and took its numbers from a SceneCapture2D actor. The framing routine posed the capture actor. Nothing in the job ever posed the viewport. So every JPEG showed whichever way the parked Dog happened to face, every figure in every gate came from a different camera at a different pose, and the two were never compared.

That is the whole explanation for the reading I could not account for: noon at 0.0118 — black at midday — in a world the viewport reads at 0.17 to 0.34. They were never the same picture. On one camera, noon now reads as a lit world and the reference the night ratios divide by is credible for the first time.

One of the seven places is dark at noon. architecture_noctis reads luma 0.0596 with 97% of the frame and 99% of its ground below the darkness threshold — at midday. That is not a night-lighting problem and no amount of moon gain will reach it. The complaint registers at night because that is when someone looked, but the gap is in daylight.

What that place is not, and what I do not yet know. I guessed twice at the cause from the layer files and was wrong both times — first that the view was aimed at empty ground, then that the nearest architecture was 11.5 km off. A cone count over every instance in the world settles it: the view holds 2,700 architecture instances, the nearest at 53 m and the bulk between 100 and 200 m. The first 50 m is bare paving, and beyond that there is a district at an entirely ordinary viewing distance. So “nothing there to light” is ruled out and the cause is not established.

The two candidates need opposite fixes, which is why guessing was the wrong move: either that district is not being drawn at 100–200 m, which is a cull-distance or streaming question, or it is drawn and genuinely unlit, which is a lighting question. A trace down the view answers it in one reading, and the sweep now takes that trace at noon for every place and reports the surfaces it finds. Until it runs, this row says only what it measured.

The cycle has a cliff exactly where the brief said not to have one

N03measured · red
2.35 stops of exposure bias, moved in 67 minutes — while Daylight sits pinned at exactly 1.0 for the 10.7 hours before it

This one comes from the source rather than the camera, which is why it is the number worth quoting. Daylight is clamp((elevation + 9) / 21) raised to 1.9, and it saturates at any elevation above 12° — so it reads exactly 1.0 from 06:38 to 17:22 and then falls the whole way to zero between 17:22 and 18:29. Sun intensity, all three fills, the sky light, the fog colour, the water colour and AutoExposureBias all ride on that one curve. The day is flat-topped and the entire transition is crammed into 67 minutes at each end.

A correction to my own first reading of this. I quoted the 18:00→19:00 drop as 93% of the day's range. That statistic is confounded and I withdraw it. Between 15:00 and 17:00 Daylight is 1.0 and the bias is −0.9 at every sample — nothing about the lighting changes at all — yet the same framing reads 0.264, then 0.182, then 0.190. That 0.082 swing is the sun's bearing sweeping from 72° to 288° and relighting this particular view. The denominator I divided by is part bearing and part brightness, so the fraction overstates the case.

And the 18:00 spike is not explained. On exposure grounds that hour should read darker than 17:00, not brighter: scene light falls 5.0× while the bias opens 1.88 stops for 3.68× of gain — a net 0.74×. It measured 1.61× brighter. Something adds light at exactly sunset that the exposure model does not account for. The fog is the candidate, since the air is thickened at both ends of the day and its inscattering takes the full warm horizon colour (1.00, 0.62, 0.33) at precisely elevation zero. A candidate, not a cause.

The hourly ladder cannot settle any of it: three samples land on a 67-minute transition. The sweep now reads every ten minutes from 16:00 to 20:00 and records Daylight and the exposure bias beside each measurement, so the model and the camera can be compared point by point.

Priority 3 · Water

Fine by day, a black hole at dusk, and the parameters say exactly why

A03measured · red A00 A01 A02green

A gate of mine had been passing this water. It went green on a night shoreline mean of 0.0314 against a 0.03 floor — while the same patch reported 99.03% of its pixels crushed to black. A mean is the wrong statistic for is it black: a few specular pixels on a dead surface carry it over the line. The gate now tests the crushed fraction, and six of nine views fail it.

A mirror with a dark base and no refraction reflects a dark sky and returns black. Nothing is broken in the shader; the surface is doing precisely what those four numbers ask of it. The rising blue/red ratio confirms it — the water still holds its hue at dusk, at a luminance too low to see.

Mascot handoff · The knee

The Companion's strategy cannot transfer, and the numbers say so cleanly

M09measured

The handoff asked whether the response knee proven on the Companion — which selected 220 at a clipped fraction of 1.79% — would work on the mascots, and was explicit that 220 must not simply be copied. It cannot be, and not because 220 is the wrong value. WonderlandTameGlow compresses only above the knee, and after the GLOW_SCALE re-baseline the brightest body glow on any skin is 13.2. Half of this table survives a root cause found after it was measured

M09high knees: arithmetic, holds knee 8, 4, 2: confounded

The other lane then found that RestColourFor was not applying the ×1000 that additive materials must carry in their Color, because those materials have no strength parameter. The per-tick animator overwrote the painter's correct value one frame later, so every additive surface on every figure has rendered black since the scene scale landed — measured on a live costume at Color (19.8, 11.5, 2.0) against a white near 25,000.

What still holds, and now on firmer ground than measurement. The knee is applied to the authored strength, before that ×1000 — Emissive = Lit × TameGlow(Strength), and RestColourFor multiplies afterwards. Authored body glow spans 3.3 to 13.2, and TameGlow returns its input unchanged below the knee. So knees at 700, 220, 64, 32 and 16 cannot touch anything, as arithmetic, whatever the render scale is. That my sweep measured them as inert is now a cross-check on the instrument rather than the finding itself — and the arithmetic says the knee first bites at 8, while the sweep independently put celestial_light's first real response at 4. The two agree.

What does not hold. At knees 8, 4 and 2 the knee genuinely compresses — and those are exactly the rows measured while additive surfaces were invisible. A knee change to a surface rendering at 0.08% of white is unmeasurable, so the low end of that table says nothing about the fixed build. Those three columns need re-running, and more skins may respond once their halos are actually visible. The verdict "inert" is safe for the high knees and withdrawn for the low ones.

The eye readings are not evidence yet

M11 M03 M04 M07 M08 M10unproven · instrument fault

Four sweep runs measured a torso. The eye locator reported it had found the eye on the mesh, and returned 24.7 uu above the actor origin — identical to four decimals on eight figures of two different heights, which cannot be eight eyes. It was a component pivot. The tell was in the data all along: face luminance ranged from 0.013 to 0.548 between skins framed identically.

StaticMeshComponent exposes no bounds property in this engine's Python, and a comment in this repository already recorded that, noting it had crashed another job. Two consecutive fixes of mine were built on it anyway. It now reads the mesh asset's bounding box through the component's world transform — which also settles whether figure bounds are in voxels or centimetres without anyone having to reason about it — and walks a ladder of candidate heights, keeping whichever produces a cell that beats its own noise floor.

Separately, and more usefully than any number I produced: the halo rings render as flat frames around a hard square with no falloff past the pane edge, because the additive material draws a constant colour across a whole cube face. That is a better account of the eyes do not glow than the sweep was going to reach, and a radial-fade replacement is in the next asset round. The eye re-run goes after it, rather than measuring a halo about to be replaced.

The blocked-start finding did not reproduce, and I withdraw it. On the rebuilt editor all three nations drive at full walk speed from a zero start error — ICARUS 643.6 uu where it had advanced 0.0. Whatever stopped it was a property of that build or that play session, not of the ground, so the topiary and the tower named as candidates are off the hook and the corridor audit proved nothing after all. It was still right not to call them the cause.

The real blocker is now the only thing left, and it is the same on all three. Each Dog walks a few metres of a 485 m route and then vanishes: ICARUS 4.5 m to waypoint 2, TARTARUS 16.2 m to waypoint 3, NOCTIS 19.4 m to waypoint 4. The run reported "the world went away" in all three cases — and that message is a lie of omission I wrote. The guard returns the same empty answer whether the world ended or the Dog was destroyed, and it blamed the world for both.

Those are opposite findings. A torn-down play session is the harness and says nothing about the route. A Dog destroyed five metres into an avenue is most likely a Dog that walked into a cell which had not streamed, dropped through the floor and was killed below the world — and that is a streaming finding about the avenues, which is exactly what QA-8 has been failing on all along. Three nations stopping at waypoints 2, 3 and 4 in one run is far more consistent with falling than with three separate teardowns, but consistency is not evidence.

So the next run says which, and proves it: it reports whether the world or the pawn went, the Dog's last known height, and how far that sits below its own leg's floor. Every one of these routes runs on the +12 shelf, so a Dog last seen thousands of units under the avenue fell, and a Dog last seen at floor height was removed some other way.

What this does not establish

  • Any eye luminance, contrast, hue-retention or clipping figure for the mascots. Four runs measured the wrong height; the knee result is unaffected because it reads frame luma and never touched the eye patch.
  • Every Skeleton Flame requirement except the eyes. No gate in the sweep reads flame motion, the low-mid-high pulse, the chest skull or the spectral side element. Unmeasured, not passing.
  • Bloom. Read from the post-process volume and reported, but not swept — so "controlled bloom" is described rather than demonstrated.
  • Why ICARUS could not move on the earlier build. It drives now, so the cause left with that build and is not recoverable from the current one. Not a world finding, and the topiary named as a candidate is cleared.
  • Whether the Dog is falling through the avenues. The most likely reading of three walks ending after a few metres, and unproven until the next run reports the pawn's last height against its leg's floor.
  • Any reading taken with a figure in frame. Every additive surface on every figure — eye halos, Celestial Light's halo edge, cub ghost tails, the Project Avatars' halos — rendered black until the RestColourFor fix, so their emissive contribution is missing from the night table and from the low-knee rows.
  • Why architecture_noctis is dark at midday. Ruled out: an empty view. Not established: whether the district fails to draw at 100–200 m or draws and is unlit. Two candidates, opposite fixes, one trace away.
  • What adds light at 18:00. The exposure model predicts that hour reads darker than 17:00; it reads 1.61× brighter. The fog's warm inscattering at elevation zero is a candidate and nothing more.
  • How much of the hourly ladder is brightness and how much is sun bearing. At constant Daylight the same framing moves 0.082, so any figure quoted as a fraction of "the day's range" is partly bearing — which is why the 93% statistic is withdrawn above.
  • DUBEL FOREST's specialisation. The canon states none, so the world marks it as needing a founder definition rather than inventing one. It is the only territory of five in that state.

Measured on the round-3 build in a single-editor window. Every reading is one composited viewport frame — the same instrument, saved beside the number it produced, so a red gate can be checked by opening the picture it names.

World exposure held throughout: EV100 pinned at 14.2 before and after every run, which matters because Wonderland's rendered white sits near 25,000 scene units and an emissive judged against a white of 25 is wrong by three orders of magnitude.
